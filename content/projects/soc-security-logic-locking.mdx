## Overview

An ongoing research project investigating Logic Locking as a hardware security mechanism for protecting Intellectual Property (IP) embedded in Systems-on-Chip (SoC). The work involves both a literature review and hands-on simulation in Vivado.

## Problem

As chip design increasingly happens across distributed supply chains, protecting proprietary logic from piracy and reverse engineering has become critical. Logic Locking is a technique that obfuscates circuit functionality until a correct key is applied — but its robustness varies significantly by implementation.

## Implementation

- **Literature review**: Surveying foundational and state-of-the-art Logic Locking schemes (random, SARLock, Strong Logic Locking, etc.)
- **Simulation**: Implementing locking mechanisms on test netlists in Xilinx Vivado
- **Evaluation**: Assessing resilience against SAT-based attacks and other known attack vectors
- **Comparison**: Benchmarking area, power, and security overhead across different locking strategies

## Current Status

Research is in progress. Simulations are running on synthesizable VHDL designs in Vivado. The goal is to produce a technical report evaluating which locking strategies offer the best balance of security and hardware overhead for practical SoC integration.
